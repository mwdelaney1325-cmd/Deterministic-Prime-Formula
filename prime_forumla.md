# Deterministic Prime Formula 

## Overview 

This document presents a deterministic algebraic construction that generates prime numbers via a unique admissibility criterion. No probabilistic methods, sieves, or analytic assumptions are used. 

The construction defines: 
- a carrier function,
- an integer readout,
- a multiplicity rule,
- and an admissibility filter. Primes arise as the admissible outputs of the system. 

--- 

## Definitions 

### 1. Domain 

Let \( i \in \mathbb{N} \) index a deterministic enumeration of primitive Pythagorean triples:

\[ T(i) = (A(i), B(i), C(i)) \] 

such that: 

\[ A(i)^2 + B(i)^2 = C(i)^2,\quad \gcd(A(i), B(i), C(i)) = 1 \] 

The ordering of the enumeration is arbitrary but fixed. 

--- 

### 2. Carrier Function

Define a rational-valued carrier:

\[ K(i) \in \mathbb{Q} \] 

computed deterministically from 

\( T(i) \).

(No geometry, trigonometry, limits, or analytic continuation is used.) 

--- 

### 3. Integer Readout

Define the integer readout:

\[ L(i) := \lfloor K(i) \rfloor \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0} \] 

--- 

### 4. Multiplicity

Define the multiplicity of an integer value

\( x \): 

\[ \mu(x) := \#\{ i \in \mathbb{N} : L(i) = x \} \] 

--- 

### 5. Admissibility Define the admissibility predicate:

\[ \mathrm{Adm}(x) := \begin{cases} 1 & \text{if } \mu(x) = 1 \\ 0 & \text{otherwise} \end{cases} \] 

--- 

## Prime Identification

Define the prime set generated by the system:

\[ \mathcal{P} := \{ x \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 2} : \mathrm{Adm}(x) = 1 \} \] 

---

## Determinism

- The carrier \( K(i) \) is deterministic.
- The readout \( L(i) \) is deterministic.
- Multiplicity \( \mu(x) \) is intrinsic to the construction.
- Admissibility depends only on multiplicity. No external testing for divisibility is performed.
  
--- 

## Verification 

Finite-range verification confirms that:
- All admissible outputs correspond to prime numbers.
- Composite numbers occur only with multiplicity

\( \mu(x) > 1 \). Verification is computational and independent of enumeration order. 

---

## Notes

- No probabilistic claims are made.
- No claims of minimality or optimality are required.
- Additional structures and interpretations are intentionally omitted.
